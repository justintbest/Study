## What this objective tests
Owners often hire their own consultants: surveyors, geotechnical engineers, hazardous-materials surveyors, commissioning agents, kitchen, AV, security, IT or lab planners, and cost consultants. Can you fold their work into the project without taking on responsibility for it? It's U/A.

> [!VERIFY] B101-2017 paragraph numbers below are from my knowledge of the 2017 edition. Confirm in your copy.

## The rules for owner's consultants
- **The owner contracts with them and coordinates them.** The owner provides the services of its consultants, coordinates them with the architect's services, and on request gives the architect their scopes (B101 Art. 5).
- **The architect coordinates its services with theirs** and may **rely** on the accuracy, completeness and timeliness of the information they furnish. But when the architect becomes aware of an error, omission or inconsistency, it must give the owner **prompt written notice** (B101 §3.1.2).
- **More than basic coordination?** "Architect's coordination of the owner's consultants" is a listed **supplemental service** (B101 §4.1.1). If it isn't in the agreement and the owner asks for it, it's an additional service.
- **Schedule allowances:** the architect's schedule includes time for the owner's consultants (B101 §3.1.3). When an owner's consultant is late, tell the owner and update the schedule.
- **Seals and responsibility:** don't seal or take responsibility for an owner's consultant's work. Identify it in the documents as theirs.
- **Hazardous materials:** the architect has no responsibility for discovering, handling or removing them (B101 §10.6). The owner provides the testing.

## Integrating their work
1. Get the **scopes** of the owner's consultants early and compare them with yours. Assign the gaps (who designs the conduit for security devices? who specifies AV backing?).
2. Put their **deliverables and review periods** in the project schedule as owner tasks.
3. Invite them to the relevant coordination meetings and include them in the distribution of documents.
4. **Commissioning agent:** comments are based on the owner's project requirements (OPR) and the basis of design. Review them with your consultants and respond in writing. Take changes that affect cost or schedule to the owner for a decision.
5. **During construction:** the owner coordinates its **separate contractors** (A201 Art. 6). The architect helps only as its agreement provides.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Quietly correcting an owner's consultant's work, such as a survey or geotechnical report. Notify the owner in writing instead.

> [!TRAP] Treating coordination of owner's consultants as automatically part of basic services.

> [!TRAP] Putting an owner's consultant's drawings under the architect's seal.
