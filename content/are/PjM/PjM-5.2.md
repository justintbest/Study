## What this objective tests
Can you run quality-control and risk-management processes that keep errors out of the documents and claims away from the firm? That includes insurance and resolving conflicts. It's A/E: choose the process, coverage or step that best controls the risk.

## Quality control (QC) program
- **Reviews at set milestones**, typically end of SD, DD, 50% CD and 95% CD. Schedule them so there is **time to make corrections** before the deadline.
- **An independent reviewer,** someone experienced who isn't on the project team, using **checklists**.
- **Log every comment,** assign who corrects it, and **back-check** that it was fixed before the set is issued.
- Coordinate the drawings and specifications (see 5.3), and keep the code analysis current (see 5.1).
- **Quality assurance (QA)** is the firm's overall system: standards, training, lessons learned. **QC** is checking a specific project.

## Risk management
| Practice | Why |
|---|---|
| Clear scope and well-written agreements | Avoid expectation gaps |
| No guarantees or warranties, and no "ensure" or "certify" language | They go beyond the standard of care and may not be insured |
| Written owner decisions and phase approvals | Proof of what was agreed |
| Review certificate language in advance (B101 §10.4) | Sign only what you can know |
| Report potential claims to your insurer promptly | Claims-made policies need notice during the policy period |
| A risk register: probability × impact, and the cost of mitigating | Spend where it reduces the most expected cost |

**Expected cost of a risk** = probability × impact. A mitigation is worth doing if it reduces expected cost by more than it costs.

## Insurance: who pays for what
| Event | Coverage |
|---|---|
| Design error causes a loss | **Architect's professional liability** (claims-made) |
| A visitor is injured at the architect's office | Architect's commercial general liability |
| The architect's employee is injured on site | **Architect's workers' compensation** |
| A worker or the public is injured by construction | **Contractor's** CGL and workers' compensation |
| Damage to the work during construction | Builder's risk (property insurance) |

## Resolving conflicts
- **Owner–architect (B101 Art. 8):** negotiate, then **mediation** is required before binding dispute resolution (arbitration or litigation, as the agreement selects).
- **Owner–contractor (A201 Art. 15):** decision by the Initial Decision Maker, then mediation, then binding resolution. As IDM the architect **doesn't favor either party** and decides in good faith.
- **Nonpayment:** the architect may suspend services on 7 days' written notice (B101 §9.1).

> [!VERIFY] B101-2017 Art. 8 and §9.1, and A201-2017 Art. 15, are from my knowledge of the 2017 editions.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Admitting fault or promising payment before notifying the professional liability insurer.

> [!TRAP] Scheduling the QC review on the day before the deadline, with no time for corrections.

> [!TRAP] Expecting the architect's PL policy to cover a worker hurt by the contractor's work. That's the contractor's coverage.
