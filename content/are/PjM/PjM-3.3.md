## What this objective tests
The owner–contractor agreement (**A101-2017**) and its general conditions (**A201-2017**) set out the architect's role during construction, even though the architect isn't a party to either. Can you apply that role? That covers site visits, rejecting work, certifying payment, submittals, changes, interpretations, completion and initial decisions on claims. It's U/A. (CE goes deeper into construction administration. PjM tests whether you understand the contract framework.)

> [!VERIFY] A201-2017 paragraph numbers below are from my knowledge of the 2017 edition. Confirm in your copy.

## The documents
- **A101-2017:** the owner–contractor agreement for a stipulated sum. It covers the contract sum, the commencement date, substantial completion and liquidated damages, progress payments and retainage, and final payment. Insurance and bonds are in its Exhibit A.
- **A201-2017:** the general conditions, which define the roles of the owner, the contractor and the architect. B101 commits the architect to provide CA as A201 describes it, and the owner must coordinate any changes to A201 with the architect (B101 §5.12).

## The architect's role under A201
| Duty | Limit |
|---|---|
| Site visits at intervals suited to the stage of construction (§4.2.2) | Not exhaustive or continuous inspection. Not responsible for means, methods or safety |
| Rejects nonconforming work, and can require testing (§4.2.6) | **No authority to stop the work.** That's the owner's right (§2.3) |
| Certifies payment within 7 days of an application (§9.4.1) | The certificate means "to the best of the architect's knowledge, information and belief." It doesn't mean the architect checked how the money was used (§9.4.2) |
| Reviews submittals (§4.2.7) | Limited to conformance with the design concept. The contractor remains responsible for deviations it didn't call out (§3.12.8) |
| Prepares change orders and CCDs, and orders minor changes (§4.2.8, §7.4) | Minor changes can't change the contract sum or time |
| Interprets the documents (§4.2.11–4.2.12) | Not liable for interpretations made in good faith. Decisions on aesthetic effect are final if consistent with the documents' intent (§4.2.13) |
| Inspects for substantial and final completion, and issues the certificates (§4.2.9, §9.8, §9.10) | Adds items it finds to the contractor's list |
| Initial Decision Maker on claims unless someone else is named (§15.2) | IDM decision → mediation → binding resolution |

## The change documents
| Document | Signed by | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Change order (§7.2) | Owner, contractor, architect | Price and time agreed |
| Construction change directive (§7.3) | Owner, architect | The work must proceed before the price is agreed |
| Order for a minor change (§7.4) | Architect | No change in contract sum or time, and consistent with intent |

## Certificate for payment calculation
**Amount due** = (work completed + suitably stored materials) × (1 − retainage) − previous certificates.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] "The architect orders the contractor to stop work." The architect rejects nonconforming work. Stopping the work is the owner's right.

> [!TRAP] Treating the architect's submittal approval as approval of an unflagged deviation, or of quantities and means and methods.

> [!TRAP] Agreeing to inspect continuously within basic services. Full-time on-site representation is a supplemental service.
