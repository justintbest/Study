## What this objective tests
Can you keep the project true to its **design objectives** (the owner's program, budget, performance targets, and the design intent) through value engineering, substitutions, owner changes, GMP pressure and construction? It's A/E: compare proposals against the stated objectives and recommend what protects them.

## Define the objectives so you can test against them
- **Program:** spaces, areas, adjacencies and priorities. Compare the design areas with the program at every phase, within an agreed tolerance (for example ±5%).
- **Performance targets:** energy use intensity, daylight, acoustics, durability or service life, certifications (LEED or similar).
- **Design intent:** key materials, aesthetic and spatial ideas, written in a **basis of design** or project criteria document.
- **Goals matrix:** each objective with its target and current status, reviewed at every milestone with the owner.

## Protecting the objectives
| Pressure | Response |
|---|---|
| **Value engineering / GMP gaps** | Compare each proposal with the objectives. Prefer savings that keep performance (equal durability, lower cost). Show the owner the trade-offs, and record the decision |
| **Owner change that conflicts with a stated goal** | Explain the effect on that goal (e.g., losing natural ventilation or a certification credit). The owner decides, and the decision is recorded |
| **Substitution requests (bidding)** | Evaluate performance data, effects on other work, dimensions, warranty and appearance. Approve by addendum only if equal |
| **Substitutions (construction)** | Only with the owner's consent, after the architect evaluates them, by change order or CCD (A201-2017 §3.4.2). Reject unapproved substitutions |
| **Submittals that drift from the intent** | Note them or reject them in submittal review |

> [!VERIFY] A201-2017 §3.4.2 (substitutions) is from my knowledge of the 2017 edition.

## How to read a VE list
For each item, ask whether it reduces **first cost** without hurting a **stated objective** such as durability, performance, daylight or a certification. Lower first cost that raises life-cycle cost, or that gives up a stated goal, isn't value. It's scope reduction, and the owner must agree to it knowingly.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Accepting the biggest VE saving without checking it against the owner's goals.

> [!TRAP] Approving a substitution on price alone.

> [!TRAP] Refusing an owner's change outright. The architect advises. The owner decides, knowing the trade-offs.
