## What this objective tests
Once the agreement is signed, can you assemble the team the contract requires? That means internal staff with the right roles, skills and licensure, and the consultants the scope needs. You also need to know which consultants are **yours** and which are **the owner's**, what they'll cost, and who is responsible for what. It's U/A: apply the criteria to a given project.

## Start from the agreement
- **Scope drives the team.** Read the services in the owner–architect agreement first.
  - B101-2017 basic services include the architect's usual **structural, mechanical and electrical** engineering (§3.1).
  - Civil, landscape, interiors, specialty consultants and similar services are **supplemental services** only if the agreement adds them (Art. 4).
- **The owner furnishes some services** (B101-2017 Art. 5): surveys, the geotechnical investigation, hazardous-material testing, and any consultants the owner hires directly. Don't hire, or pay for, what the owner is supposed to provide.
- **Delivery method and schedule matter.** A fast-track CMc project may need early-release packages and more coordination time. Design-build may put the architect under the design-builder.

> [!VERIFY] B101-2017 §3.1 (structural, MEP in basic services) and the Art. 4/Art. 5 allocations are from my knowledge of the 2017 edition. Confirm against your copy.

## Internal roles
| Role | Typical responsibility |
|---|---|
| Principal-in-charge | Client relationship, contract, major decisions, quality oversight |
| Project manager | Scope, fee, schedule, staffing, communication, consultant management |
| Project architect | Technical lead: code analysis, documentation, coordination, often CA |
| Designers / technical staff | Design development and production |
| Specification writer / QC reviewer | Project manual, independent review |
| CA staff | Submittals, RFIs, site visits, pay applications |

**Responsible control:** a licensed architect must direct and supervise the work and seal only what was prepared under their responsible control (NCARB Model Rule 5.1). An unlicensed project lead can run production, but a licensed architect controls and seals.

## Choosing consultants
**Selection criteria:**
- experience with the building type;
- licensure in the project's jurisdiction;
- capacity in the project's timeframe;
- past coordination quality;
- **insurance that meets the prime agreement's requirements**;
- BIM and technical capability;
- location, for site visits;
- fee, but never fee alone.

**Agreements:** use C401-2017, so the consultant's duties and insurance mirror the prime agreement (see 3.2).

**Assign the gaps explicitly.** Some items fall between disciplines. Put them in a responsibility matrix:
- site lighting (electrical or landscape?)
- stormwater (civil or plumbing?)
- fire alarm and fire protection (electrical, a fire-protection engineer, or design-build by the contractor?)
- low-voltage and AV
- signage
- envelope commissioning

## Fees
The architect's fee normally **includes** its consultants' fees.
- Budget the consultants first: get their proposals before committing to a fee.
- Then confirm what remains for the architect's own work.
- Added consultants at the owner's request become additional services. The usual practice is the consultant's fee times a markup, plus the architect's coordination time.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Hiring the geotechnical engineer or surveyor yourself. Under B101 those are the **owner's** responsibilities.

> [!TRAP] Choosing a consultant mainly on fee, or one whose insurance is below what the prime agreement requires.

> [!TRAP] Assuming an unlicensed project architect can seal the documents. Only the licensed architect in responsible control can.

> [!TRAP] Leaving items that span disciplines unassigned. Gaps become change orders.
