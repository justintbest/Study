## What this objective tests
Can you track the estimated construction cost against the owner's budget at each phase, spot problems early, and recommend what to do? It's A/E: read an estimate, apply contingency and escalation, compare it with the budget, and choose among value analysis, scope changes, alternates or a budget change. (PPD 5.2 covers evaluating the estimate itself. This objective is about **managing** to the budget over the project.)

## Budget vocabulary
- **Construction budget (cost of the work):** what the owner expects the construction contract to cost. B101-2017 Art. 6 defines the cost of the work as including the contractor's general conditions, overhead and profit. It excludes the architect's fee, land, financing and owner contingencies.
- **Project budget:** the construction budget **plus** soft costs: land, fees, FF&E, permits and testing, financing, the owner's contingency.
- **Design contingency:** covers design that isn't drawn yet. It's large at SD (about 10–15%) and shrinks to a few percent at the end of CDs.
- **Escalation:** price change from the estimate date to the **midpoint of construction**.
- **Owner's construction contingency:** held by the owner for changes during construction. It's separate from the design contingency.

## Estimating methods by phase
| Phase | Typical method |
|---|---|
| Programming / SD | Cost per square foot (or per unit, bed or seat) from similar projects |
| DD | Systems or assemblies (foundations, structure, envelope, MEP…) |
| CD | Quantity takeoff × unit prices |

## Calculations to know
- **Escalated cost** = cost today × (1 + annual rate × years to the midpoint of construction).
- **Estimate to compare with the budget** = documented scope × (1 + design contingency) × (1 + escalation).
- **Maximum construction cost from a project budget:** subtract the fixed soft costs, then divide by (1 + the percentage-based soft costs).
- **Meeting the budget with alternates:** pick the combination that just closes the gap and keeps the owner's priorities.

## When the estimate exceeds the budget
- B101-2017 §6.5: the architect recommends adjustments to **size, quality or budget**, and the owner cooperates.
- Typical tools:
  - **reduce scope:** area or program elements;
  - **value analysis:** systems and materials that cost less but meet the performance needed;
  - **deductive or additive alternates** in the bid documents;
  - **a budget increase** by the owner.
- **Reconcile two estimates line by line** (for example, the architect's estimator against the CM's). Compare quantities, unit prices, scope assumptions (allowances, alternates) and escalation dates. Don't just average the two.

> [!VERIFY] B101-2017 Art. 6 definitions and §6.5 are from my knowledge of the 2017 edition. Contingency ranges are typical practice values, not code requirements.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Escalating only to the construction **start**, or all the way to completion. Use the **midpoint**.

> [!TRAP] Deleting the design contingency to "meet" the budget. The risk is still there.

> [!TRAP] Calling the total project budget the construction budget. Soft costs come out first.
