## What this objective tests
Can you plan and manage the approvals the project needs from **authorities having jurisdiction**: the building permit, phased and deferred approvals, alternative methods, inspections, certificates of occupancy, appeals, and other agencies? It's U/A. ARE questions use the **IBC 2021** (Chapter 1, Scope and Administration). Local jurisdictions often amend Chapter 1, so on a real project check the local version.

## Getting the permit (IBC 2021)
| Topic | Section | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Permits required | §105 | Permit before construction. Some minor work is exempt |
| Fees | §109 | Usually based on the building's valuation |
| Submittal documents | §107 | Prepared by a registered design professional where state law requires it |
| **Design professional in responsible charge** | §107.3.4 | Coordinates the submittal, including deferred items |
| **Deferred submittals** | §107.3.4.1 | Items such as sprinkler shop drawings or delegated-design elements, listed on the documents and approved by the building official |
| **Phased approval** | §107.3.3 | The building official may issue a permit for part of the work (e.g., foundations) before the full plans are approved, at the holder's risk |
| **Alternative materials, design and methods** | §104.11 | Approved if shown equivalent, with tests, research reports or analysis |
| Special inspections | §1704–1705 | Statement of special inspections prepared by the registered design professional |

## During and after construction
- **Inspections** (§110) at the required stages. The contractor schedules them.
- **Certificate of occupancy** (§111). A **temporary certificate** (§111.3) may allow part of the building to be occupied safely before completion.
- **Disagreements:** first meet with the building official and present the code analysis. If that fails, use the **board of appeals** (§113). Zoning relief (variances) goes to the zoning or planning body, not the building department.

## Other approvals to plan for
- Zoning and site-plan approval
- Health department (food service, pools)
- Fire department access and hydrants
- Utility service applications
- Environmental:
  - **EPA NPDES stormwater** coverage for construction that disturbs **1 acre or more** (40 CFR 122.26);
  - state or local erosion control.
- Historic review where applicable
- FAA review near airports

## Accessibility: not a local approval
The 2010 ADA Standards are civil-rights law, enforced by the U.S. Department of Justice and through lawsuits. **A building permit doesn't certify ADA compliance.** A local review checks only the locally adopted code (IBC Chapter 11 / ICC A117.1). For new construction, the 2010 Standards apply when the permit application was certified complete on or after **March 15, 2012** (28 CFR 36.406).

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Assuming the plan reviewer's approval means the design meets the ADA.

> [!TRAP] Going straight to the board of appeals before meeting with the building official.

> [!TRAP] Forgetting that phased approval is at the owner's risk if later reviews require changes.
