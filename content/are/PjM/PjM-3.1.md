## What this objective tests
This is the biggest PjM objective (10–14%). Can you **administer** the owner–architect agreement once it's signed? That means knowing what's in basic services and what isn't, handling supplemental and additional services, billing by phase, getting phase approvals, and responding when the delivery method, budget or schedule changes. It's A/E: apply the agreement's terms to a situation and pick the response the agreement supports. (Negotiating the agreement is PcM 2.3.)

> [!VERIFY] Paragraph numbers below are from my knowledge of B101-2017. Confirm them in your copy (free to candidates through the NCARB Record).

## Three kinds of services
| Kind | What it is | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic** (Art. 3) | Phase services from SD through construction. Includes the usual structural, mechanical and electrical engineering (§3.1) | Basic compensation |
| **Supplemental** (§4.1) | Listed in the agreement when it's signed: programming, civil, landscape, interiors, detailed cost estimating, on-site representation, coordinating the owner's consultants, multiple bid packages, fast-track, commissioning, record drawings… | Agreed when signed |
| **Additional** (§4.2) | Comes up after signing | §11.3 rates, plus a schedule adjustment |

## Additional services: two procedures
1. **Notify, explain and get written authorization before proceeding** (§4.2.2). Examples:
   - a material change in the project (size, quality, complexity, schedule, budget, **procurement or delivery method**);
   - changes in codes after the documents are prepared;
   - the owner's late decisions;
   - public hearings;
   - work after a fire or other casualty during construction.
2. **Provide the service to avoid delay during construction, and notify the owner promptly** (§4.2.3). Examples:
   - out-of-sequence submittals;
   - RFIs whose answers are already in the documents;
   - change orders that need evaluation or document revisions;
   - many claims;
   - substitutions.

   The owner can say a service isn't needed. The architect is paid for work done before that notice.
- **Limits on CA services** (§4.2.4): the number of submittal reviews, site visits and completion inspections. Anything beyond those is an additional service.
- **Time limit** (§4.2.5): services that run past the agreed duration through no fault of the architect are additional services.

## Phase billing and approvals
- Basic compensation is split by phase (§11.5). The usual split is **SD 15%, DD 20%, CD 40%, procurement 5%, construction 20%**. Bill by phase as work is completed.
- At the end of each phase, submit the documents and the estimate update, and **get the owner's approval** before starting the next phase (§3.2–§3.4).
- **Percentage-of-cost fees:** if parts of the project are deleted, the architect is still paid for the services performed on them (§11.6).

## Budget clauses (Art. 6)
- The architect's estimates use conceptual methods. A detailed estimate is a supplemental service or the owner's (§6.3).
- **§6.4:** if bidding hasn't started within **90 days** after the CDs are submitted, through no fault of the architect, the budget is adjusted for market price changes.
- **§6.5:** if an estimate exceeds the budget at any point, the architect recommends adjustments to size, quality or budget.
- **§6.6:** if the lowest bona fide bid exceeds the budget, the owner may:
  - approve a budget increase;
  - rebid or renegotiate;
  - terminate;
  - revise the scope with the architect;
  - choose another agreed alternative.
- **§6.7:** if the owner chooses to revise the scope, the architect modifies the CDs **without additional compensation**. The exception is an overage caused by market conditions the architect couldn't reasonably anticipate; that work is an additional service. Modifying the documents is the limit of the architect's responsibility.

## Termination
- The owner may terminate for convenience on 7 days' written notice (§9.5).
- The architect is then paid for:
  - services performed;
  - reimbursable expenses;
  - termination expenses, including anticipated profit on the services not performed (§9.6–§9.7);
  - a licensing fee if the owner keeps using the instruments of service (§11.9).

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Starting a §4.2.2 service (a change in delivery method, a code change, a hearing) before getting written authorization.

> [!TRAP] Assuming redesign after high bids is always free. Market conditions the architect couldn't anticipate make it an additional service.

> [!TRAP] Moving into the next phase on a verbal "keep going" without the owner's approval of the phase.
