## What this objective tests
Can you make sure the project team identifies and follows the laws, regulations and codes that apply: the adopted building code, zoning, federal accessibility and fair-housing law, and other federal requirements? Can you also make sure the code analysis is complete and the permits are obtained? It's U/A. (PPD and PDD test code application in depth. PjM asks whether the **process** catches it.)

## Which rules apply
- **The adopted edition governs,** not the newest model code. Check the code the jurisdiction has adopted and any local amendments, and whether a new edition takes effect before the permit application.
- **All applicable rules must be met.** Zoning and the building code are separate. Where they differ, the more restrictive limit controls in practice. Within the IBC, when sections conflict, the **most restrictive** governs, and a specific requirement governs over a general one (IBC 2021 §102.1).
- **Federal laws apply on their own**, whatever the local code says:
  - **ADA**: Title II for state and local government facilities (28 CFR 35.151). Title III for public accommodations and commercial facilities (28 CFR 36.406). The 2010 Standards apply to new construction when the permit application was certified complete on or after March 15, 2012.
  - **Fair Housing Act** design requirements for **covered multifamily dwellings** first occupied after March 13, 1991: in buildings of **4 or more units with an elevator, all units**; in buildings of 4 or more units without an elevator, **ground-floor units** (42 U.S.C. §3604(f); 24 CFR 100.201).
  - **NHPA Section 106**: a federal agency must consider effects on historic properties before approving federal funds or a license for a project (54 U.S.C. §306108).
  - **EPA NPDES** construction stormwater coverage when 1 acre or more is disturbed (see 4.4).

## The code analysis
| Item | IBC 2021 chapter |
|---|---|
| Occupancy classification | Ch. 3 |
| Allowable height and area | Ch. 5 |
| Construction type | Ch. 6 |
| Fire-resistance and separations | Ch. 7 |
| Sprinklers and alarms | Ch. 9 |
| Occupant load and egress | Ch. 10 (Table 1004.5 load factors) |
| Accessibility | Ch. 11 / ICC A117.1-2017 |
| Energy | IECC (as adopted) |

- Start it at **SD**, refine it in **DD**, and finish it in **CDs**, with a summary sheet in the permit set.
- **Occupant load factors** (Table 1004.5):
  - business: 150 gross sf;
  - assembly, unconcentrated (tables and chairs): 15 net sf;
  - assembly, concentrated (chairs only): 7 net sf.
- **Exits:** 1–500 occupants need 2 exits, 501–1,000 need 3, and more than 1,000 need 4 (Table 1006.3.3).

## Who gets the permit
- B101: the architect **assists** the owner with filings for governmental approvals.
- A201-2017 §3.7.1: unless the contract documents say otherwise, the **contractor** secures and pays for the building permit and the inspections normally obtained after the contract is signed.

> [!VERIFY] A201-2017 §3.7.1 is from my knowledge of the 2017 edition. Confirm in your copy.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Designing to the newest model code instead of the edition the jurisdiction adopted.

> [!TRAP] Assuming walk-up apartments are exempt from the Fair Housing Act. Ground-floor units in buildings of 4 or more units are covered.

> [!TRAP] Treating local code approval as proof of compliance with federal law.
