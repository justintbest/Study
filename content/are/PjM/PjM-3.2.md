## What this objective tests
When the architect hires consultants, the architect–consultant agreement (**C401-2017**) has to line up with the owner–architect agreement (the **prime agreement**). Can you use C401 to set the consultant's scope, schedule, insurance, payment and changes so that the architect can keep its promises to the owner? It's U/A.

> [!VERIFY] C401-2017 details below (flow-down, payment timing, termination) are from my knowledge of the 2017 edition. Confirm against your copy.

## Flow-down: the key idea
- Under C401, the consultant takes on toward the architect the obligations the architect took on toward the owner, to the extent they apply to the consultant's services. The architect, in turn, takes on toward the consultant the obligations the owner took on toward the architect.
- So the architect gives the consultant the **relevant terms of the prime agreement**: scope, schedule, standard of care, insurance, CA duties and dispute terms.
- **The owner has no contract with the consultant.** The architect stays responsible to the owner for its consultants' work.

## What to define in the C401
| Item | Make it match the prime agreement |
|---|---|
| Scope by phase | Deliverables for each phase. Consultant CA duties: site visits, submittal reviews in its discipline, punch lists |
| Schedule | Consultant milestones **before** the architect's deadlines, leaving time to coordinate and review |
| Insurance | Professional liability and other coverage at least what the prime agreement requires |
| Standard of care | The ordinary skill and care of professionals in that discipline. No guarantees or warranties the prime agreement doesn't require |
| Compensation | Fee, rates and reimbursables. When and how the consultant is paid relative to the owner's payments to the architect |
| Changes | Consultant additional services need the architect's written authorization, normally after the owner authorizes them |
| Ownership | The consultant owns its instruments of service and licenses them for this project only |
| Termination | Lets the architect terminate the consultant if the prime agreement ends |

## Additional services
1. The owner asks for a change (a new wing, a code change).
2. Get the **owner's written authorization** under the prime agreement, including the consultant's fee times the agreed markup.
3. Then **authorize the consultant in writing.**

If the architect authorizes consultant work the owner hasn't approved, the architect may end up paying for it.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Accepting a consultant's lower insurance limits than the prime agreement requires. The architect carries the gap.

> [!TRAP] Telling an owner to sue or deal with the consultant directly. The owner contracts with the architect.

> [!TRAP] Letting consultant milestones fall on the same day as the architect's deadline. Leave time to coordinate.
