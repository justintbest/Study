## What this objective tests
The schedule isn't just design time. Can you identify and evaluate everything else that sets the dates: **client reviews and approvals, regulatory submissions, testing, long-lead items, funding deadlines and seasonal limits**? It's A/E: figure out which constraint controls, schedule backward from fixed dates, and fit approvals into the plan.

## Criteria that drive the schedule
| Criterion | What to plan for |
|---|---|
| **Owner reviews and approvals** | Review time at each phase. Board or committee meeting cycles and agenda deadlines. Phase approval before the next phase starts |
| **Owner-furnished information** | Survey, geotechnical report, program, equipment lists. They must arrive **before** the design work that depends on them |
| **Regulatory submissions** | Zoning or planning hearings (notice periods, appeal periods), building permit review and resubmittal cycles, health and fire reviews, utility applications |
| **Testing** | Geotechnical borings and lab tests, envelope or curtain-wall mock-up tests, material testing, commissioning |
| **Long-lead items** | Utility transformers, switchgear, elevators, specialty equipment. Some must be ordered early, sometimes by the owner |
| **Fixed dates** | Funding or grant deadlines, lease expirations, school openings, fiscal years |
| **Seasonal limits** | Earthwork or roofing weather windows, school-session restrictions, operating seasons |

## How to use them
- **Schedule backward from a fixed date.** Subtract each step (move-in, construction, award, bidding, permit) to find the latest date for each deliverable. Then add float.
- **Run parallel reviews in parallel,** and find the longest chain. That's the one that controls.
- **Count resubmittal cycles.** Permits rarely issue on the first review, so plan for review, corrections, re-review and issuance.
- **Owner-furnished items** go in the schedule as owner tasks with due dates. Remind the owner early. B101-2017 assigns surveys and geotechnical services to the owner.
- **Testing needs time before fabrication.** Specify mock-ups and tests, and their sequence, so the contractor's schedule includes them. The architect doesn't schedule the contractor's testing lab.

## Phase approvals
B101-2017 has the architect get the owner's approval before moving from one phase to the next. Schedule those approvals as real tasks, with the owner's actual decision process: a board, a committee or a lender.

> [!VERIFY] Phase-approval wording and §3.1.3 schedule allowances are from my knowledge of B101-2017. Confirm in your copy.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Assuming the building permit always controls. A long utility lead time, a hearing's appeal period or a mock-up test can control instead.

> [!TRAP] Scheduling an owner deliverable (the geotechnical report) to start when the design needs it. It must be **finished** by then.

> [!TRAP] Assuming a public board can approve within a week. Check its meeting cycle and posting deadlines.
