## What this objective tests
Can you look at a firm's people and tools and decide what it needs **now** and **next**? Expect scenarios like these: a backlog spike, a key person leaving, a new market (healthcare, energy modeling, BIM), or a software or hardware purchase. You pick the response that best fits the firm's business model and finances. It's an A/E objective, so the "right" answer usually weighs several factors rather than reciting a definition.

## Key concepts

### Staffing to the workload
- **Start from the forecast.** Compare *backlog* (signed work not yet earned) and *probable* new work against staff capacity, measured in hours by role. Hiring decisions follow the gap between demand and capacity.
- **Short spike vs. lasting growth.**
  - Temporary peak → overtime, contract (agency) staff, or outsourcing a discrete task. Overtime is fine briefly, but it is costly and burns people out.
  - Sustained growth or a new service line → permanent hire.
- **Match skills to phase.** Schematic design needs designers. Construction documents need production and technical staff. Construction administration needs people with field and contract-administration experience. Staffing plans should change as projects move through phases.
- **Utilization** (chargeable hours ÷ total hours) shows whether people are under- or over-loaded. A firm-wide figure that is high *and* rising usually means it's time to add capacity.

### Hiring, assignments, evaluations
- **Hiring criteria** should come from the role: skills, licensure or AXP status, software, experience with the building type, and how well the person collaborates. Criteria must be job-related and non-discriminatory (see 1.2 for the employment laws).
- **Staffing assignments** balance project needs, cost (billing rate vs. fee available), development goals, and continuity with the client.
- **Evaluations** should be regular, documented, and tied to stated expectations. Documentation protects the firm if a termination is ever challenged, and it drives development plans.
- **Professional development:**
  - mentorship and support for AXP and ARE candidates;
  - licensure continuing education, which most jurisdictions require, usually with a health/safety/welfare (HSW) portion;
  - cross-training to reduce key-person risk.

### Capital expenditures (hardware, software, training)
Evaluate every purchase against strategy and the numbers:
- **Total cost of ownership** = purchase or subscription + implementation + training + lost productivity while learning + ongoing support and upgrades.
- **Payback period** = investment ÷ annual net benefit (savings or new profit).
- **Return on investment (ROI)** = (benefit − cost) ÷ cost.
- **Lease vs. buy**: leasing keeps cash in the firm and makes upgrades easier. Buying can cost less over a long life but ties up capital.
- **Timing and cash**: a sound investment can still be the wrong move if it drains working capital the firm needs to make payroll.
- **Strategic fit**: tools that support the current business model, or a deliberate move into a new practice area, beat "nice to have" purchases. Examples: energy modeling to offer more sustainable design services, or reality capture for renovation work.

## Definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Backlog | Value of contracted work not yet earned/billed; often expressed in months of work |
| Capacity | Available productive hours of current staff |
| Utilization rate | Chargeable (billable) hours ÷ total hours worked or paid |
| Contract staff | Temporary workers from an agency; flexibility without long-term commitment |
| Payback period | Time for an investment's benefits to repay its cost |
| Key-person risk | Over-dependence on one individual's knowledge or relationships |

## Rules of thumb
- Fix **process and training** before buying more tools. Software doesn't fix a workflow problem.
- **Hire for the long term, flex for the peak.**
- A new practice area needs **people, tools, and credentials** together. Software alone doesn't make the firm competent in a building type, and taking on work beyond your competence raises ethics and standard-of-care problems (see 1.3, 1.4).
- Phase big technology rollouts with a pilot project and a training plan.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Hiring permanent staff for a temporary peak. When the scenario says the spike lasts a few months, the best answer is usually overtime, contract staff, or outsourcing.

> [!TRAP] Choosing the cheapest option instead of the best fit. A/E items reward the option that matches the firm's strategy and the problem described: staff skills, timeline, cash position.

> [!TRAP] Forgetting training and productivity loss in technology decisions. The license fee is rarely the biggest cost.

> [!TRAP] Treating utilization as "higher is always better." Very high utilization leaves no time for marketing, training, or quality control, and it predicts burnout.
