## What this objective tests
How do project type, client type and contract terms shape the firm's **risk, legal exposure and insurance coverage**? How do you resolve conflicts and manage quality across the practice? It's a U/A objective: recognize the risk-shifting clause, the insurable vs. uninsurable promise, and the right step on the dispute-resolution ladder.

## Sources of exposure
- **Contract claims:** breach of the agreement's terms, such as scope, schedule or deliverables.
- **Tort claims:** negligence, which means breaching the standard of care (see 1.4), and sometimes negligent misrepresentation.
  - Many states limit purely economic losses to contract remedies (the *economic loss doctrine*). The rules vary by jurisdiction.
- **Third parties:** people with no contract with you, such as future owners, occupants or contractors, may still sue in tort.
- **Time limits:**
  - A *statute of limitations* runs from when the claim accrues or the injury is discovered.
  - A *statute of repose* sets an absolute cutoff measured from substantial completion, regardless of discovery.
  - B101-2017 §8.1.1 adds a contractual outer limit: claims must be started within the period applicable law allows, and **in any case no more than 10 years after substantial completion**.

## Contract terms that shift risk
| Clause | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Indemnification** | Fine *to the extent caused by* the architect's negligence (B101-2017 §8.1.3 is negligence-based). Avoid **broad-form** indemnity (covering others' fault) and **duty to defend** language, since defense duties arise before fault is decided and are often uninsurable |
| **Limitation of liability** | Caps the architect's total liability, e.g., at the fee or a dollar amount. It must be negotiated; enforceability varies by state |
| **Mutual waiver of consequential damages** | Bars claims for indirect losses such as lost profits, lost rent and financing costs (B101-2017 §8.1.4). Good for the architect. Watch for one-sided versions |
| **Standard of care** | Keep "ordinary skill and care." Strike warranties and guarantees (1.4) |
| **Certifications** | Qualify the wording and limit it to your scope (B101-2017 §10.4) |
| **Insurance requirements** | Must match coverage the firm actually carries and that is available (B101-2017 §2.5) |
| **Third-party beneficiaries** | B101-2017 §10.5 says nothing in the agreement creates rights for third parties |
| **Assignment** | Neither party may assign without the other's written consent. The owner can typically assign to its lender (B101-2017 §10.3) |

**Insurability test:** professional liability policies cover *negligence*. Promises that go beyond negligence, such as guarantees, warranties, broad indemnities, liquidated damages and duty-to-defend obligations, are typically **contractual liability** that the policy excludes.

## Client and project types
- **Higher-risk project types:** for-sale residential condominiums (homeowners' associations sue often), additions to and renovations of older buildings, highly technical buildings (labs, healthcare), and fast-track schedules.
- **Higher-risk client types:**
  - first-time or inexperienced owners;
  - financially weak or speculative developers;
  - owners who resist sound advice;
  - owners who insist on onerous contract terms.
- **Public clients** bring procurement rules, standard public contract forms with non-negotiable terms, and open-records exposure.

## Resolving disputes: the ladder
1. **Direct negotiation.** Fastest and cheapest, and it preserves the relationship.
2. **Mediation.** A neutral *facilitates* settlement. It's **non-binding** and confidential.
   - B101-2017 §8.2 makes mediation a **condition precedent** to binding dispute resolution.
   - The parties share the mediator's fee equally. Mediation takes place where the project is located unless the parties agree otherwise.
3. **Binding dispute resolution.** In B101-2017 §8.2.4, the parties check a box:
   - **Arbitration:** private, binding, limited appeal, and the arbitrators may be construction-savvy. Joining other parties can be an issue.
   - **Litigation:** public, with full discovery and appeals; slower and costlier.
   - **Other.**

   If no box is checked, **litigation** is the default.

Other mechanisms:
- the **Initial Decision Maker** under A201-2017 §15.2 for owner–contractor claims (the architect by default);
- dispute review boards on large projects;
- project neutrals.

Some states require a **certificate of merit** (an affidavit from a design professional) before a claim against an architect can proceed. Rules vary by state.

## Quality management
- **QA** = the firm's processes: standards, checklists, training, peer review.
- **QC** = checking the deliverables: milestone reviews, coordination checks, constructability reviews, code reviews.
- **Communication and documentation:** meeting minutes, confirmation of verbal decisions in writing, field reports, change logs.
- **Claims handling:** report potential claims or circumstances to the insurer promptly. Don't admit liability. Don't promise to pay. Preserve records.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Choosing "agree to defend and indemnify the owner for all claims." Indemnify only to the extent caused by your negligence, and avoid duty-to-defend language.

> [!TRAP] Thinking mediation produces a binding decision. The mediator facilitates. Only arbitration (or a court) binds.

> [!TRAP] Thinking B101's default binding method is arbitration. It was in pre-2007 AIA forms. Since 2007 the default is **litigation** unless arbitration is checked.

> [!TRAP] Calling the architect at fault when a claim arrives. Notify the insurer first. Admissions can void coverage.

> [!VERIFY] B101-2017 paragraph numbers (§§8.1.1–8.2.4, 10.3–10.5) are from my knowledge of the 2017 edition. Confirm them in your copy.
