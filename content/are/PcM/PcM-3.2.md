## What this objective tests
You need to:
- tell the delivery methods and their contract families apart;
- know which agreement fits which relationship;
- estimate the **impact and risk of changes** to contracts.

It's an A/E objective and a big one (10–14%). Expect scenarios like "the owner wants cost certainty early and an accelerated schedule; which method and documents fit?"

## Delivery methods at a glance
| Method | Relationships | Architect's position | Strengths | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Design-bid-build (DBB)** | Owner–Architect (B101), Owner–Contractor (A101 + A201) | The owner's independent adviser and agent during construction | Competitive price at bid; clear roles; documents complete before pricing | Longest schedule; cost unknown until bids; adversarial change orders |
| **Construction manager as adviser (CMa)** | Owner–CM adviser (C132), Owner–Architect (B132), Owner–Contractor(s) (A132) | A peer adviser alongside the CM | Construction expertise early; owner keeps control | The CM carries no cost risk; more parties to coordinate |
| **Construction manager as constructor (CMc / CM at risk)** | Owner–CMc (A133 with a guaranteed maximum price (GMP), A134 without), Owner–Architect (B133) | Designs while the CM gives preconstruction input, then administers construction | Early cost input; GMP; enables fast-track | The GMP is often set on incomplete documents; contingency disputes; the CM's interest shifts once the GMP is set |
| **Design-build (DB)** | Owner–Design-builder (A141); Design-builder–Architect (B143) | Often a **consultant to the design-builder**, owing duties to the DB entity, **not** the owner | Single point of responsibility; speed | The owner loses an independent advocate; quality depends on performance criteria; architect is far from the owner |
| **Integrated project delivery (IPD)** | Multi-party or transitional agreements (A195/B195; C191 multi-party) | A collaborative partner sharing risk and reward | Early collaboration; BIM; aligned incentives | Requires trust and experienced teams; novel risk allocation |
| **Multiple prime** | Owner holds several trade contracts | Coordinates documents for separate bid packages | Can save markup; phased starts | The owner (or a CM) carries coordination risk |

**Fast-track** = overlapping design and construction (bid packages released in sequence). It's a scheduling *strategy*, often used with CMc or design-build, not a delivery method in itself.

**Bridging** = the owner's architect prepares preliminary design and performance criteria. A design-builder then completes the design and builds it.

## Owner–contractor payment bases
- **Stipulated sum (lump sum):** A101. Most cost certainty at bid; the contractor carries cost risk.
- **Cost of the work plus a fee, with a GMP:** A102 for general use, A133 for CMc. Open-book accounting; savings may be shared.
- **Cost plus fee without a GMP:** A103 / A134. The owner carries cost risk.
- **Unit price:** for quantities that can't be known in advance (earthwork, piles).

## Owner–architect agreement family (B-series)
| Document | Use |
|---|---|
| **B101-2017** | Standard owner–architect agreement for traditional delivery (pairs with A101/A201) |
| B102-2017 | No predefined scope; attach a scope document such as B201 |
| B103-2017 | Large or complex projects |
| B104-2017 | Abbreviated, for limited scope or moderately complex projects |
| B105-2017 | Short form for small residential or small commercial projects |
| B132-2019 / B133-2019 | CMa / CMc editions |
| **B143-2014** | Design-builder–architect |
| **B195-2008** | IPD transitional owner–architect |

Other key documents on the ARE:
- **C401-2017:** architect–consultant. It flows the architect's prime-agreement obligations down to its consultants.
- **A201-2017:** General Conditions. Most owner–contractor forms adopt it by reference, and B101 relies on its definitions.
- **A701-2018:** Instructions to Bidders.

## Estimating the impact of contract changes
Ask four questions about any proposed modification:
1. **Who takes on new risk?** For example, adding "the architect shall ensure compliance" moves risk to the architect.
2. **Is it insurable?** Negligence-based terms are; warranties, broad indemnities and liquidated damages generally aren't (see 2.2).
3. **Is it coordinated?** Agreements in the same family share definitions. B101 and A201 must match, for example on the architect's role, the number of site visits, and the Initial Decision Maker. Changing one document without the other creates conflicts.
4. **What does it do to scope, fee and schedule?** Adding services or compressing the schedule should change the fee.

Modify AIA documents **visibly**: use the AIA software's tracked changes or a separate exhibit. Don't retype the form. AIA licensing terms and the parties' trust both depend on seeing exactly what changed.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Assuming the architect owes duties to the owner in design-build when engaged by the design-builder under B143. The contract runs to the design-builder.

> [!TRAP] Thinking a CM adviser guarantees cost. Only a CMc (with a GMP) carries cost risk.

> [!TRAP] Pairing the wrong documents: B101 with A133 (use B133 for CMc), or B101 for design-build (use B143).

> [!TRAP] Calling fast-track a delivery method. It's a scheduling approach used *within* a delivery method.

> [!VERIFY] Edition years for B102–B105, B132/B133, A132/A134 and C132 are from my knowledge, not NCARB's list. NCARB's list includes A101-2017, A133-2019, A141-2014, A195-2008, A201-2017, A701-2018, B101-2017, B143-2014, B195-2008, C401-2017, G701-2017, G702-1992, G703-1992, G704-2017 and G709-2018.
