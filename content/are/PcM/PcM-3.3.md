## What this objective tests
Evaluate a **potential project** for the risk it brings to the *practice*, and decide how to **mitigate** that risk. It's an A/E objective.

> [!VERIFY] Third-party reports say the April 2026 update removed the "rewards" element from this objective. The current wording in the October 2026 Guidelines focuses only on risks and mitigation. Expect questions on identifying and reducing risk, not weighing profit potential.

## Where project risk comes from
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Client** | Inexperienced, financially weak or unfunded; unclear decision-maker; history of disputes; unrealistic expectations |
| **Project type** | For-sale condominiums; healthcare and labs; renovations of old buildings; a type new to the firm |
| **Site** | Poor soils, contamination, flood or wildfire exposure, unknown existing conditions, difficult access |
| **Budget and schedule** | A budget that doesn't fit the program; a compressed schedule; fast-track |
| **Delivery method** | Design-build with the architect as a subconsultant; early GMPs; multiple primes |
| **Contract terms** | Broad indemnity, warranties, liquidated damages, low liability caps *for the owner*, unlimited services |
| **Regulatory** | Complex approvals, historic review, environmental review, public hearings |
| **Team** | Consultants new to the firm; the owner's separately hired consultants; an inexperienced contractor |
| **Firm capacity** | A project that would overload staff or depend on one key person |
| **Geography** | Out-of-area work: licensure, unfamiliar local codes and conditions, longer travel for construction administration |

## Mitigation toolbox
Match the tool to the risk:
- **Avoid:** decline the project, or decline the risky part of the scope.
- **Reduce:**
  - experienced staff, partners or consultants;
  - QA/QC and peer review;
  - realistic schedules;
  - clear assumptions;
  - phased authorization (e.g., a feasibility study first);
  - an owner's contingency;
  - investigations: surveys, geotechnical reports, hazardous-materials testing.
- **Transfer:**
  - negotiated contract terms: negligence-based indemnity, limitation of liability, consequential damages waiver;
  - insurance (practice policy, or a project-specific policy bought by the owner);
  - consultants contracted under C401 terms that mirror the prime agreement.
- **Share:** a joint venture or teaming arrangement with another firm, or IPD risk-sharing.
- **Accept, with eyes open:** price the risk (fee, contingency), document decisions, and monitor.

### Client financial risk
- Ask about funding. For private developers, confirm financing.
- Use a **retainer** or initial payment (B101-2017 §11.10.1).
- Bill monthly, and watch receivables.
- Keep the **suspension and termination for nonpayment** rights (B101-2017 §9.1).
- The architect's copyright license is conditioned on payment (B101-2017 Art. 7).

### Risk from incomplete information
Turn unknowns into **services** (existing-conditions survey, programming), **owner responsibilities** (B101-2017 Art. 5: surveys, geotech, testing), or **stated assumptions**. Don't absorb them silently.

## Thinking at the practice level
One risky project can threaten the whole firm. Consider:
- whether a worst-case claim could exceed insurance limits or wipe out a year's profit;
- whether a large project would consume so much capacity that the firm neglects other clients;
- the firm's **portfolio balance**: don't concentrate too much high-risk work at once.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Choosing "buy more insurance" for every risk. Insurance doesn't cover uninsurable promises (warranties, LDs), and it does nothing about capacity or expertise gaps.

> [!TRAP] Accepting all onerous terms to win a prestigious project. A/E answers usually negotiate, team, or decline.

> [!TRAP] Treating unknown conditions as the architect's risk to absorb. Assign them through owner responsibilities, services, or assumptions.

> [!TRAP] Skipping client due diligence because the project is attractive. Client financial capability is a primary risk.
