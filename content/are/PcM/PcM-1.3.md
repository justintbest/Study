## What this objective tests
Given a situation, what's the ethical and lawful response? Expect scenarios involving:
- plagiarism, copyright or intellectual-property infringement
- exaggerating qualifications or credit
- defamation (libel or slander) of competitors
- conflicts of interest, gifts
- client requests that cross a line

The ARE expects you to know **which rulebook applies** and **what the conduct rules require**.

## Three layers of rules
| Source | Who it binds | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| **State licensing law and board rules** | Every licensed architect in that jurisdiction | License discipline (fine, suspension, revocation) |
| **NCARB Model Rules of Conduct** (revised July 2023) | Nobody directly. It's a model that boards adopt in whole or in part. NCARB Certificate holders certify each year that they know it | Through each board's adopted rules |
| **AIA Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct** (2024 edition on the ARE) | **AIA members only** | AIA National Ethics Council. Penalties: **admonition, censure, suspension, or termination of membership**. Admonitions stay confidential; the others are made public. Not license action |

**NCARB Model Rules: six rules (July 2023)**
1. Competence
2. Conflict of Interest
3. Full Disclosure
4. Compliance with Laws
5. Signing and Sealing Documents
6. Further Obligations to the Profession and the Public (AXP supervisors)

**AIA Code: three tiers.**
- **Canons** are broad principles.
- **Ethical Standards (E.S.)** are aspirational goals.
- **Rules of Conduct** are *mandatory*; violating one is grounds for discipline.

There are six canons:
1. General Obligations
2. Obligations to the Public
3. Obligations to the Client
4. Obligations to the Profession
5. Obligations to Colleagues
6. Obligations to the Environment

## Core conduct principles
Paraphrased, with rule numbers: NCARB = Model Rules (July 2023); AIA = 2024 Code.

- **Competence.** Take on only work you, with your consultants, have the knowledge and skill to perform (NCARB 1.3; AIA 3.102).
- **Law.** Don't violate the law in your practice. Don't counsel or assist a client in conduct you know, or reasonably should know, is fraudulent or illegal (NCARB 4.1, 4.3; AIA 2.101, 2.106).
- **Public safety over client wishes.** Suppose a client or employer decides, against your advice, something that violates building laws and would materially and adversely affect public safety.
  - **NCARB 3.5:**
    1. refuse to consent;
    2. report it to the official who enforces the building laws;
    3. if more such decisions are likely, **terminate your services** on the project, unless you can get it resolved some other way.
  - **AIA 2.105:**
    1. **advise** the client or employer against the decision;
    2. refuse to consent;
    3. report it to the building official, unless it can be resolved some other way.
  - Both rules concern the safety of the **finished project**. Jobsite safety during construction is the contractor's obligation.
- **Conflicts of interest.**
  - Don't perform services with an undisclosed conflict, meaning a financial or other interest in the project or a relationship that could affect your judgment. The conflict must be **fully disclosed and waived in writing by all parties** (NCARB 2.3). AIA 3.201 similarly requires consent, after full disclosure, from everyone who relies on your judgment.
  - Don't take compensation from more than one party on a project unless it's disclosed and waived in writing by all parties (NCARB 2.1).
- **Supplier payments.** Never solicit or accept compensation from material or equipment suppliers for specifying or endorsing their products (NCARB 2.2).
  - **Disclosure doesn't cure it.** This rule has no waiver.
  - Customary, reasonable business hospitality and product education aren't "compensation."
- **Gifts and officials.**
  - Never offer or give a payment or gift intended to influence an official about a project (NCARB 4.5; AIA 2.102).
  - When *you* serve in a public capacity, don't accept gifts intended to influence you (NCARB 2.6; AIA 2.103).
- **Impartiality.** When acting as interpreter of the contract documents or judge of contract performance, decide impartially (NCARB 2.4; AIA 3.202).
- **Truthful representation.**
  - No misleading, deceptive or false statements (NCARB 3.1; AIA 4.103).
  - Don't misrepresent your or your firm's qualifications, or overstate your role in work you claim credit for (NCARB 3.3, 3.4; AIA 4.201).
  - Don't mislead clients about results you can achieve (AIA 3.301).
- **Seal only what you control.** Sign and seal only technical submissions prepared under your **responsible control** (NCARB 5.1; AIA 4.102). NCARB allows two exceptions:
  - incorporating reviewed information from consultants, manufacturers or contractors (5.2);
  - adopting prototypical documents after verifying local compliance (5.3).
- **Confidentiality.** Don't knowingly disclose information that would hurt your client, or that you were asked to keep confidential, unless the Code or the law allows or requires it (AIA 3.401).
- **Reporting others.**
  - Report known violations of the jurisdiction's practice laws to the **board** (NCARB 3.9).
  - AIA members with substantial information about another member's serious violation must file a complaint with the National Ethics Council (AIA 4.101).
- **Colleagues and employees.**
  - No harassment or discrimination (AIA 1.401). Under NCARB 4.6, an employer found to have violated employee-protection laws is subject to board discipline.
  - Respect others' contributions (AIA 5.301).
  - Departing staff don't take firm materials without permission (AIA 5.302). Firms don't unreasonably refuse copies of non-confidential work the person did (AIA 5.303).
- **Fee competition is not unethical.** The AIA Code's antitrust statement says competitive bids or price quotations, discounts, and free services are not, in themselves, unethical.

## Intellectual property and plagiarism
- **Copyright.** Drawings and the **architectural work itself** are protected once fixed in tangible form.
  - An *architectural work* is the building design: its overall form and the arrangement and composition of spaces and elements, but **not individual standard features** (17 U.S.C. §101, §102(a)(8)).
  - **Registration isn't required for protection**, but it **is required before suing** for a U.S. work (§411(a)).
  - Statutory damages and attorneys' fees generally require registration **before the infringement or within three months of first publication** (§412).
- **Work made for hire.** An employee's work within the scope of employment belongs to the employer. A commissioned work counts as "for hire" only in nine listed categories with a signed writing, and **architectural works aren't among them** (§101). So a freelancer's design belongs to the freelancer unless they **assign it in writing** (§201).
- **Not protected:** ideas, general styles, standard features. **Photos of a building visible from a public place are allowed** (§120(a)).
- **Plagiarism vs. infringement.**
  - *Plagiarism* is presenting someone else's work as your own: a truthfulness problem (NCARB 3.1, 3.4; AIA 4.201) even when no copyright is violated.
  - *Infringement* is a legal violation of exclusive rights. Copying a competitor's published plans can be both.
- **Using another firm's documents.** Get a license or written permission. Owners usually have only a limited license to use instruments of service for their own project (B101-2017 Art. 7).

## Defamation: libel and slander
- **Libel** = written or published defamation. **Slander** = spoken defamation.
- It requires a **false statement of fact** that harms reputation. **Truth is a defense**; honest opinion based on disclosed facts is generally protected.
- Ethically, architects must not knowingly make false statements of material fact in a professional capacity (AIA 4.103) or make misleading or false statements (NCARB 3.1). Compete on your own merits.

## Rules of thumb
- If the client wants something **illegal or unsafe**, the answer is never "comply and document."
- If a choice would **misrepresent** anything (credit, qualifications, licensure, cost, schedule), it's wrong.
- Conflict? **Disclose fully and get written waivers from all parties**, or step away. Supplier kickbacks can't be waived at all.
- "AIA member?" decides whether the AIA Code applies. "Licensed?" decides whether board rules apply.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Thinking the AIA Code can cost you your license. Only the state licensing board can discipline a license. The AIA can only act on membership.

> [!TRAP] Listing past-firm projects as your own without explanation. You must describe your actual role and credit the firm of record.

> [!TRAP] Assuming no copyright registration means no protection. Protection exists on fixation. Registration affects your ability to sue and the remedies available.

> [!TRAP] Assuming a freelancer's drawings belong to the firm automatically. Architectural works can't be "commissioned works made for hire." You need a written assignment.

> [!TRAP] Thinking disclosure makes a supplier commission acceptable. NCARB 2.2 has no waiver.

> [!TRAP] Calling competitive fee bidding or free services "unethical." The AIA Code says they aren't, in themselves.
