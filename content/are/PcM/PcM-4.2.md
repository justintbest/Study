## What this objective tests
How does a firm **standardize** the way it designs, coordinates and documents projects, and how do those processes change for **different project types**? It's an A/E objective: pick the policy, standard or resource that best fits the situation.

## Practice-wide policies and standards
- **Office standards manual:**
  - drawing and CAD/BIM standards
  - title blocks
  - standard details
  - specification templates
  - file naming and folder structure
  - QA/QC procedures
  - document control
- **Design principles and policies:** a firm-wide design philosophy, sustainability commitments, and design review at milestones.
  - Examples: the AIA Framework for Design Excellence, or tracking energy performance under the AIA 2030 Commitment.
- **Records retention:** keep project records at least through the applicable statute of repose and the agreement's claim period (B101-2017 §8.1.1 sets a 10-year outer limit). Many firms keep key records longer.
- **Knowledge management:** lessons learned, a technical library, post-occupancy evaluation feedback, and peer review.

## Industry standards for documentation
| Standard | What it organizes |
|---|---|
| **U.S. National CAD Standard (NCS)** | Drawing set organization, sheet identification, layers, symbols. Includes the **Uniform Drawing System (UDS)** |
| **Sheet ID (UDS)** | Discipline designator + sheet type + sequence. E.g., **A-101**: A = architectural; 1 = plans; 01 = sequence |
| **MasterFormat (CSI)** | Specifications organized by *work results* in 50 divisions. E.g., 03 Concrete, 07 Thermal & Moisture Protection, 08 Openings, 09 Finishes, 23 HVAC, 26 Electrical. The ARE references the 2023 edition |
| **SectionFormat / PageFormat (CSI)** | Each section has three parts: **Part 1 General, Part 2 Products, Part 3 Execution** |
| **UniFormat (CSI/ASTM)** | Organizes by building *elements and systems*: A Substructure, B Shell, C Interiors, D Services, E Equipment & Furnishings, F Special Construction & Demolition, G Building Sitework. Used for early cost estimates and preliminary descriptions |
| **OmniClass** | A broad classification system for the built environment, used in BIM data |
| **BIM level of development (LOD)** | How reliable a model element is. **100** conceptual → **200** approximate geometry → **300** precise geometry → **350** interfaces with other systems → **400** fabrication detail → **500** field-verified (as-built) |

UDS sheet-type digits:
- 0 general
- 1 plans
- 2 elevations
- 3 sections
- 4 large-scale views
- 5 details
- 6 schedules and diagrams
- 7–8 user-defined
- 9 3D representations

Specification methods:
- **Prescriptive (descriptive):** describes materials and installation in detail.
- **Performance:** states the required results; the contractor chooses how.
- **Reference standard:** cites an industry standard, such as ASTM.
- **Proprietary:** names products, either closed (no substitutes) or open ("or equal").

## Coordination processes and resources
- **BIM execution plan:** who models what, when, and to what LOD. Model ownership and data exchange are often covered by AIA E203 with G202.
- **Clash detection and coordination meetings** for MEP-structure conflicts.
- **Consultant coordination:**
  - a responsibility matrix covering scope gaps and overlaps;
  - scheduled model and drawing exchanges;
  - coordinated specifications, with no duplicate or conflicting sections.
- **Checklists** at SD, DD and CD milestones for code, accessibility, life safety, envelope and constructability.
- **Peer review / independent QC** before issuing documents for permit or bid.

## Adjusting to project type
| Project type | Process emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Healthcare** | Regulatory agencies and design guidelines (e.g., FGI Guidelines); infection control; heavy MEP coordination; phased work in occupied facilities |
| **Historic preservation** | The Secretary of the Interior's Standards: **Preservation, Rehabilitation, Restoration, Reconstruction**. Section 106 review when there is federal involvement. Existing-conditions documentation; specialist consultants |
| **Federal** | Agency design standards (e.g., GSA P-100), security requirements, specification formats set by the agency |
| **K-12 / higher education** | Stakeholder engagement, public approvals, campus standards |
| **Repetitive (retail, multifamily prototypes)** | Prototype standards, site-adaptation procedures, version control |
| **Renovation / occupied buildings** | Phasing plans, existing-conditions verification, hazardous materials, protecting occupants |
| **Design-build / fast-track** | Bid package planning, performance specifications, early release documents, tight change control |

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Mixing up MasterFormat and UniFormat. MasterFormat organizes *specifications* by work results (divisions). UniFormat organizes by *building elements* (systems) for early estimates.

> [!TRAP] Confusing restoration with rehabilitation. Rehabilitation allows compatible alterations for a new use. Restoration returns a building to its appearance at a specific period.

> [!TRAP] Thinking a higher LOD number means more drawings. LOD measures the *reliability* of model information.

> [!TRAP] Thinking performance specs mean less work for the architect. They need carefully defined performance criteria and close submittal review.

> [!VERIFY] UDS sheet-type numbers and LOD definitions follow NCS and BIMForum conventions as I know them. Confirm against the current NCS version and the BIMForum LOD Specification.
