## What this objective tests
Can you read a firm's numbers and choose actions that improve its position? Expect A/E items built on firm financial statements, which appear in PcM case studies:
- compute or compare utilization, overhead rate, break-even rate and net multiplier;
- recognize cash-flow problems that sit alongside reported profit;
- pick the action that fits the firm's strategic priorities.

The ARE gives formulas when they're needed, but you must know what each metric *means*.

## The building blocks
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Gross revenue** | Everything billed, including reimbursables and consultants |
| **Net operating revenue (NOR)** | Gross revenue − consultant fees − reimbursable expenses. What the firm *earns with its own labor* |
| **Direct labor (DL)** | Salary cost of hours charged to projects. Also called direct salary expense |
| **Indirect expenses (overhead)** | Everything else: non-billable salaries, benefits and payroll taxes, rent, insurance, software, marketing, admin |
| **Direct personnel expense (DPE)** | Direct salary + mandatory and customary benefits. Some contracts bill as a multiple of DPE |

## Core ratios (know them cold)
- **Utilization rate** = chargeable hours ÷ total hours.
- **Overhead rate** = total indirect expenses ÷ direct labor.
  - Example: $750k ÷ $500k = **1.50** (150%).
- **Break-even rate (break-even multiplier)** = (direct labor + overhead) ÷ direct labor = **1 + overhead rate**.
  - Example: 2.50. Every $1 of direct labor must bring in $2.50 of net revenue just to break even.
- **Net multiplier (effective multiplier)** = NOR ÷ direct labor.
  - If the net multiplier is above the break-even rate, the firm makes a profit. If it's below, the firm loses money.
- **Profit (before tax)** = NOR − (direct labor + indirect expenses).
- **Profit margin** = profit ÷ NOR.
- **Billing rate** = hourly salary × target multiplier. For example, $45/hr × 3.2 = $144/hr.
- **Days sales outstanding (DSO) / aging** = A/R ÷ (annual revenue ÷ 365). This is how long it takes to get paid.
- **Backlog (months)** = signed but unearned fees ÷ average monthly revenue.
- **Current ratio** = current assets ÷ current liabilities. Below 1.0 means short-term obligations exceed short-term resources.

## Financial statements
- **Income statement (P&L):** revenue and expenses over a *period*. It shows profit.
- **Balance sheet:** a *snapshot* of assets = liabilities + equity. It shows liquidity and solvency.
- **Cash flow statement:** where cash actually came from and went.
- **Cash vs. accrual accounting:**
  - *Cash basis* records income when it's received and expenses when they're paid.
  - *Accrual basis* records income when it's earned and expenses when they're incurred. Accrual gives the truer picture of performance, so you need **both** a P&L and cash tracking.

## Reading the signals
| Symptom | Likely issue | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Profitable P&L but can't make payroll | Cash flow: slow collections, large unbilled work in progress | Bill promptly (monthly), follow up on aging A/R, require retainers, arrange a line of credit |
| Net multiplier ≈ break-even rate | Thin margins | Raise rates or multipliers, improve utilization, cut non-essential overhead, change the fee strategy |
| Low utilization | Too much non-billable time or not enough work | Business development, reassign staff, review staffing levels |
| A/R over 90 days rising | Collection risk | Call clients, suspend services per contract (B101-2017 §9.1, with 7 days' notice), check client creditworthiness up front |
| One client is 50% of revenue | Concentration risk | Diversify clients and markets |
| Thin backlog | Future revenue gap | Ramp up marketing and pursuits now; plan staffing carefully |

## Strategic priorities
Financial health isn't just maximizing profit this year. A firm may *deliberately* accept lower margins to:
- invest in a new market;
- train staff;
- build a reputation.

An A/E answer should fit the firm's stated priorities and protect **liquidity**.

## Rules of thumb (vary by firm; verify)
- Typical multipliers on direct salary in the ~2.5–3.5 range. Overhead rates often ~1.3–1.8.
- Technical staff utilization is often 80–90%+. Principals are much lower. Firm-wide figures are often ~60–70%.
- Bill monthly. The longer you wait, the slower the cash.

> [!VERIFY] The ranges above are rules of thumb, not standards. Benchmarks vary by firm size and market (see the AIA Firm Survey). The ARE tests the concepts and the math, not specific benchmark values.

## Common exam traps
> [!TRAP] Including consultant fees and reimbursables in the multiplier. Use **net** operating revenue for the net multiplier.

> [!TRAP] Confusing the overhead rate with the break-even rate. Break-even = 1 + overhead rate.

> [!TRAP] Treating profit as cash. A profitable firm can run out of cash when receivables age.

> [!TRAP] Cutting all marketing to boost short-term profit when the backlog is thin. That fixes this quarter and starves next year.
